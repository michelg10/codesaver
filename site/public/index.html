> what will my screen look like while I'm away?

  • ● Code from your own GitHub repos, typed out live in SF Mono.

    └ A glowing typing head and a block cursor. Settled lines dim as they age, and new lines clip whatever they land on, so it never reads as a grid.

  • ● A spinner that never finishes its thought.

    └ ✽ Sprokbooking… (2m 7s · ↓ 1.6k tokens · still coding with xhigh effort)

    └ Made-up verbs run Claude Code's real request lifecycle: a beat of latency, then streaming with elapsed time and a ↓/↑ token counter, up the “still coding” ladder at the real 10, 20, 30 and 45 seconds, until it rests on ✻ Sprokbooked for 2m 7s. Then the next one starts.

  • ● A clock in a TUI box.

    └ Chunky half-block digits with ticking seconds, the date as a comment on the top rail. 12 or 24 hours, or off.

  • ● Every display, one machine.

    └ One shared session spans all your screens. Pure black means OLED and mini-LED panels stay dark around the text, and a frame takes about 1–2 ms to draw.

/* ignition */

Your desktop blows apart into the code.

Optional. A small helper keeps a fresh capture of your screen while you're idle, so the saver's first frame is your own desktop. Then two shockwaves run through it on the character grid. The first pixelates it into one color per cell, stamps real code over the mosaic and flings characters through the grid. The second wipes it to black, and the saver ignites in its wake.

  • · Idle trigger: a short warning line appears beside your pointer first.
  • · Hot corner: it goes off at once.
  • · Needs Screen Recording permission. Without it, the saver simply starts plain.
  • · The capture never leaves your Mac and is deleted the moment you're back.

/* live widgets */

What your agents are doing while you're not watching.

Optional. One panel per running Claude Code session: the project, and what it's doing right now. Thinking, running a tool, waiting on your permission, done. Active sessions float above resting ones. In the other corner, whatever is playing in Music or Spotify, its cover redrawn in text.

A demo of the widgets: three session panels cycle through Thinking, Bash running, Permissions required, Responding and Done, and a now-playing panel shows a track with a progress bar and a cover drawn in text characters.

/* faq */

Does it slow my Mac?

No. It only runs while the screensaver is up, and a frame takes about 1–2 ms to draw. The background refresh runs about once a day, only when plugged in, at the lowest priority macOS offers.

Is my code uploaded anywhere?

No. CodeSaver downloads your repos from GitHub to your Mac and reads them from there. It talks to GitHub and, once a day while the app is open, checks for a new version. That's all.

Does it work without GitHub?

Yes. Until you connect an account, it types a small built-in sample of code. Connecting is just a username for public repos.

How do I update it?

Download the new version and drag it into Applications, replacing the old one. Then open CodeSaver once, so it can re-select itself as your screensaver. While the app is open, it checks for a new version once a day and links back here when there is one.

How do I uninstall it?

Open CodeSaver, go to its Advanced section, and click Uninstall.

Is this made by Anthropic?

No. CodeSaver is an independent, unaffiliated homage to Claude Code, made by one developer and released under the MIT license.
